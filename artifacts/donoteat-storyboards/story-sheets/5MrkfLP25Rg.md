## Premise and central argument

Donoteat01 builds Franklin’s Good Hill Water Works while explaining how municipal water supply evolved from buckets and wells to aqueducts and pumped reservoirs. The presenter models the fictional installation on Philadelphia’s Fairmount Water Works, an inspiration explicitly acknowledged at the end. His central argument is that impressive, relatively clean and inexpensive public infrastructure can still distribute its immediate benefits very unequally: water subscriptions and construction charges favor prosperous households, while long working hours prevent the poor from enjoying the celebrated pleasure grounds. He nevertheless treats the waterworks as an overall improvement, contrasting its eventual public-health and development benefits with environmental damage and the absurdly long wait for benefits to “trickle down.” Historical and technical accounts below describe his presentation, not independently verified findings.
