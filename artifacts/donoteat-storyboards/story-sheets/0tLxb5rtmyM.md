## Premise and central argument

Donoteat begins by treating enthusiasm for trains as an overpowering addiction, then uses Franklin's new rail infrastructure to explain steam locomotion, wheel arrangements, early passenger and freight operations, railway charters, land speculation, incompatible gauges, and construction labor. His central argument is that early railroads offered transformative speed without the advertised comfort or safety, while their private-company development encouraged land grabs, monopoly protection, legal loopholes, and dangerous street running. The technical and historical explanations below are the presenter's account. Gameplay is a parallel construction montage, interrupted by diagrams, photographs, maps, and jokes; the episode ends by celebrating the completed rail city in an extended cinematic tour.

- The cinematics alternate high aerials with low forward rail rides. Trains move through the waterfront and streets with smoke trailing behind; the exact camera paths are uncertain.
