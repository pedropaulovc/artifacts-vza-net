## Premise and central argument

While expanding Franklin and establishing outlying towns after the preceding episode's water-supply project, donoteat01 uses a comparatively non-urbanist episode to establish the political economy behind future urban developments. He distinguishes capital-L liberalism from the American liberal/conservative divide, presents Locke's property-centered social contract and Smith's free-market concepts, then argues that their promises of universal freedom have historically coexisted with exploitation and exclusion. In his account, neoliberal privatization, austerity, and hostility to unions revive rather than transcend that tradition. A prison-strike solidarity announcement before the introduction makes the stakes immediate: the freedom and labor rights excluded from the philosophical promise are not merely historical abstractions. These are the presenter's interpretations and historical claims, not independently verified findings.
