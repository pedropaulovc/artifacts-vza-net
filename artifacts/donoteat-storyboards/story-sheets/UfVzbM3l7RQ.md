## Premise and central argument

The presenter asks how Greenwood, Tulsa’s prosperous Black neighborhood, accumulated wealth, how an alleged elevator incident became a racist massacre, and why the neighborhood ultimately disappeared despite rebuilding afterward. His explanation begins with O. W. Gurley’s land purchases and unusually local circulation of money under segregation, then follows Dick Rowland, Sarah Page, the courthouse crisis, white mob organization, fire and detention. He argues that Black wealth did not insulate residents from racist power, that community self-defense was necessary despite overwhelming odds, and that Interstate 244 and Oklahoma State University’s campus inflicted more lasting spatial damage than the 1921 attack because they made formerly residential and commercial land unavailable for rebuilding. These are the presenter’s historical and political assertions, not independently established findings of this sheet.
