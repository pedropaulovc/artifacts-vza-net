## Premise and central argument

Because *Franklin* will take time to reach modern urban issues, the presenter introduces a shorter, mostly North American planning series. He begins with a seemingly practical *Cities: Skylines* tutorial—how to size realistic parking lots—and turns its escalating land consumption into an argument against the policies producing them. In his account, municipal parking minimums oblige buildings and even changes of use to supply generous amounts of off-street parking, raising development costs and encouraging demolition of existing homes and businesses. He argues that abundant private parking can coexist with complaints about scarce cheap curbside spaces, making reform politically difficult. The practical lesson is therefore double-edged: make game parking realistic, but recognize why those realistic requirements can damage actual cities.

## Ending/payoff and explicit connections

The payoff is not that the three lots are bad game builds; it is that successful compliance with seemingly ordinary parking rules can produce disproportionate land use and demolition. The first lot is enormous yet arguably insufficient, the garage sacrifices the neighborhood it ostensibly serves, and the condo conversion trades homes and shops for a modest number of spaces. The closing request is to carry the planning question out of the game into real life.
