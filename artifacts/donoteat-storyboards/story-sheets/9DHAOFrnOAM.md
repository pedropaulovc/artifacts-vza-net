## Premise and central argument

Donoteat and bsquiklehausen use Cities: Skylines cinematics, historical maps, photographs and comic inserts to race through roughly 300 years of American urban development, mostly through East Coast examples. A believable city’s shape should reflect how and when it grew. Walking cities cluster around waterfront jobs; rail and streetcar access change land values and draw development outward. Postwar subdivisions follow cars, single-use zoning and publicly financed highways, with racial policy shaping who can move where. Redevelopment changes those inherited forms again. Their advice to builders is to understand *when* and *why* a neighborhood formed before laying out its streets and buildings or adding decorative props.
