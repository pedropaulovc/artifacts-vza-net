## Premise and central argument

Donoteat and bsquiklehausen use Cities: Skylines cinematics, historical maps, photographs and comic inserts to race through roughly 300 years of American urban development, largely through East Coast examples. Their argument is that a believable city’s overall shape should express the era and mechanisms that produced it: walking distance from waterfront jobs, land speculation financing rail, streetcar corridors, postwar car-dependent subdivisions, single-use zoning, publicly financed highways, racialized suburbanization, and present-day redevelopment. The practical payoff is a building lesson: identify *when* and *why* a neighborhood formed before placing its streets and buildings, rather than relying entirely on decorative props. These are the presenters’ historical interpretations, not independently verified findings of this sheet.
