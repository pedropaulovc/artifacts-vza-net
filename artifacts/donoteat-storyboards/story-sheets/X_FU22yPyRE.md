## Premise and central argument

Franklin Hospital, the University of Franklin, and a temporary national capital look like civic progress. In the presenter's account of the fictional city's 1790s setting, wealthy hospital donors also benefit from quarantining poor, mentally ill, and immigrant residents while treatment remains dangerous and ineffective. The university mainly trains Protestant clergy, and the new government's universal-sounding rights depend in practice on race, sex, and property. Beautiful grounds accompany unequal access and control. The presenter also anticipates education and medicine driving redevelopment, and promises later rants about their modern debt-funded forms. These are his historical interpretations.
